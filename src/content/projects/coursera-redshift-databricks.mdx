## The engagement

Coursera had run their analytics on Amazon Redshift for years. They wanted to move to Databricks Delta Lake on Unity Catalog. The architecture and platform choices were made by their team and our architect by the time I came in.

What I owned was the PM side of getting it across the line: kickoff, sprint cadence, weekly status to the account team and client sponsor, the RAID log, financial tracking, the cutover comms, and the closeout on the way out.

## What I actually did

**Kickoff and setup.** Picked the engagement up in the sales channel at SOW signature. Read the SOW and scoping doc. Set up the project's tracking — start/end dates, rates, roles, budget, PO and invoice coding, resources scheduled — and got a second PM's review on the plan before anything went external. Stood up the shared drive folder using the standard structure and the project's Slack channel. Ran the 45-minute internal kickoff with the account team and delivery lead. Drafted and rehearsed the external kickoff deck (from the current template, not someone else's customer's). Ran the 1-hour client kickoff with their product owner and sponsor. Welcome email went out the same day with the system access asks — on data work that's always the first thing to block delivery.

**Weekly cadence.** Standups, backlog grooming with the team and the client product owner, sprint planning, review/retro. Status report to the account team, delivery lead, and the client sponsor on the same day each week. 30-minute internal leadership prep before the client-facing leadership meeting (timeline, accomplishments, blockers, risks, collaboration topics). Kept the color status honest — flipped to yellow on access delays the client side owned, even when it was easier to leave it green.

**Blockers and escalation.** Access requests to the source systems were the recurring story. The team can't migrate what they can't see, so most of the early-engagement RAID log was access escalations through the client product owner. Two stalled long enough that I escalated to the client sponsor and our AE in the same conversation rather than wait — both unblocked the next week.

**Cutover.** Worked the cutover plan with the architect and the client engineering team — comms timeline, rollback window, who was paged, downstream owner sign-offs.

**Closure.** Ran the standard close-out: final invoicing reconciled, final status report out, NPS link sent, knowledge transfer to the client team taking the platform forward, access removed across the team.

## What was real about it

A two-region engineering pod (US + India) on a fixed-price engagement with a date in the SOW. Engineering owned the technical migration. I owned the project landing — and on fixed-price, that meant being very honest very early about anything that could put the date at risk.

## What I'd do differently

I underestimated how much PM bandwidth a two-region pod would take in the first three weeks. The standup and grooming cadence I'd planned for one pod had to be doubled, and the other program I was covering felt the squeeze. Next time I'd either run thinner ceremony in the second region during the ramp window, or arrange coverage on the other program while a new pod is setting up.
