## The engagement

A T&M Snowflake build for a global industrial manufacturer's China factory operations. The architecture and platform decisions were the engineering team's call. What made this one different on the PM side was the geometry: a 10.5-hour timezone spread between the US delivery lead and the client site, with Lunar New Year sitting three weeks into a six-week window.

## What I actually did

**Kickoff sequence.** Picked the assignment up in the sales channel. Read the SOW. Set up the project's financial tracking (rates, roles, the resource schedule for the US-India team and the client-site liaison), got a second PM's review on the plan before anything went external. Built the Slack channel and shared drive folder. Ran the 45-minute internal kickoff with the account team and delivery lead. Scheduled the external kickoff at a time that worked for everyone — the China site joined first thing in their day, the US lead late afternoon theirs. Welcome email out on day one with the access asks.

**Async-first cadence by week two.** Within the first ten days it was clear the live weekly client leadership meeting wasn't going to hold — two of the three regions weren't reliably in the same room. So I moved the cadence to async-first: a written status update at a fixed point in each region's day, a shared decision register people could comment on instead of waiting for a meeting, and the live leadership meeting moved from weekly to fortnightly. The sponsor was on board after the fact, but a 1:1 with the China-site product owner before announcing it would have made it a co-decision rather than a heads-up. That's the one I'd do differently.

**Re-baselining around Lunar New Year.** Three weeks in, I walked the AE and the client sponsor through a re-baselined schedule: vendor development front-loaded, client validation back-loaded, no open critical-path work during the holiday. Required a transparent conversation about reshaping the schedule but it meant nobody hit the holiday with anxiety.

**Financials and status.** Financial tracking clean every week, burn against SOW checked against the PMO's own reporting, color status honest. Two weeks ran yellow on access issues the client team owned, then back to green.

**Closure.** Standard closure checklist — final invoicing reconciled, final status, NPS sent, KT to the client's ongoing support team, access removed.

## What was real about it

The work itself was a standard Snowflake build the engineering team owned. The PM work was design choices about cadence and the calendar — choosing async-first early enough that decision-lag didn't compound, and treating Lunar New Year as a constraint to schedule around in week 3 rather than a thing to absorb in real time.

## What I'd do differently

The cadence change should have been a 1:1 conversation with the client product owner before the leadership meeting, not at it. They were on board after the fact, but it briefly felt like the rhythm had changed without consultation. 15 minutes of pre-conversation would have made it a co-decision.
